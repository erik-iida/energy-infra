# GridEconomics daily - Fri 2 Oct 2026

**Headline.** Romania had the widest storage spread of the CEE/SEE zones: TB4 256 EUR/MWh against a regional median of 205, on a baseload of 162. Montenegro was at the 99th percentile of its own 90 days (TB4 211), and RO was at the 94th.

**What stood out.** RO, HU, SK, HR and RS all showed TB4 above 200 and zero negative hours. That points to expensive evening peaks rather than a solar glut: the spread is paid at the peak, not at midday.

Greece is the exception. It had 8 negative hours, but its solar capture rate was only 10% (wind was 114%): solar was priced out of the market, and wind earned more than baseload.

The Baltics sit apart. LT and LV baseload was 56 EUR/MWh against 140-200 elsewhere in the region, the Baltic TB4 was only about 85, and solar capture there was above 100%.

**Next 24 h (Sat 3 Oct).** The auction puts GR at the widest TB4, 215 EUR/MWh. The curve peaks at 209 at 22:00 CET and bottoms at -20 at 13:00, so negative hours are expected again.

**Fundamentals (last 30 days).** Installed wind and solar relative to mean load is highest in LT (3.2x) and DE-LU (3.2x), lowest in RS and SK. Across 16 zones that ratio has almost no rank correlation with mean TB4 (-0.04) but a weak positive one with negative hours (+0.28): in this window, installed wind and solar explain how often prices go negative, not how wide the spread is.
