# GridEconomics daily - Fri 2 Oct 2026

**Headline.** Greece had 8 hours of negative day-ahead prices, the most in its last 90 days: wind and solar together produced 94 % of its electricity demand that day.

**Price decoupling.** The widest price gap between connected zones was Poland and Lithuania: average power price 165 against 56 €/MWh, 66 % apart, and the two prices differed in all 24 hours. Latvia and Estonia priced with Lithuania, and southern Sweden (SE4) was 65 % above it, so the Baltic states split from both of their western neighbours. In coupled markets prices only separate when the cross-border capacity is fully used, so the LitPol and NordBalt links were the bottleneck all day. In the south, Greece (121 €/MWh) and Southern Italy (215 €/MWh) were 44 % apart.

**Next 24 h (Sat 3 Oct).** The auction puts Greece at the widest TB4, 215 €/MWh. Its prices bottom at -20 €/MWh at 13:00 CET and peak at 209 €/MWh at 22:00, so negative hours are expected again.

**Fundamentals (last 30 days).** Wind output covered the largest share of consumption in Lithuania (51 %), Germany-Luxembourg (30 %) and Greece (26 %); solar in Greece (35 %), Bulgaria (35 %) and Lithuania (25 %). Across 16 zones, the wind + solar share of consumption has a weak positive rank correlation with negative-price hours (+0.28) and none with the mean TB4 (-0.03): in this window, more wind and solar meant more negative hours, not wider spreads.
